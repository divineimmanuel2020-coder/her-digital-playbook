The Digital Girl Glossary

Words, decoded 🔎

Digital and business terms in plain language, with examples you can actually use.

  • Digital ProductDigital Business
    Definition
    A product that exists only in digital form and is delivered electronically.
    In plain English
    Something you make once and sell over and over — like an ebook, template, or course.
    Example
    A $19 Notion template for meal planning.
    Why it matters
    Digital products can earn income without ongoing physical inventory.

    Related: Offer, Niche

    Learn more: How to Create and Sell a Digital Product

  • Online BusinessDigital Business
    Definition
    A business that operates primarily or entirely over the internet.
    In plain English
    A business you can run from your laptop, wherever you are.
    Example
    A freelance design business run entirely through email and video calls.
    Why it matters
    Lower overhead and location flexibility compared to many traditional businesses.

    Related: Digital Product, Business Model

    Learn more: Build Your Online Empire

  • Business ModelDigital Business
    Definition
    The core plan for how a business creates and captures value.
    In plain English
    The basic explanation of how your business actually makes money.
    Example
    A subscription model where customers pay monthly for ongoing access.
    Why it matters
    Clarifying this early prevents building something with no real path to income.

    Related: Offer, Online Business

  • OfferDigital Business
    Definition
    The specific product or service a business sells, along with its terms.
    In plain English
    What you’re actually selling, spelled out clearly enough that someone could say yes to it.
    Example
    "$150 for a 5-page website, delivered in 2 weeks."
    Why it matters
    A vague idea isn’t sellable — a specific offer is.

    Related: Niche, Target Audience

    Learn more: Offer Builder template

  • NicheDigital Business
    Definition
    A specific, focused segment of a market.
    In plain English
    Your specific lane — not "marketing," but "email marketing for small bakeries."
    Example
    A copywriter who only writes for wellness coaches.
    Why it matters
    Specificity tends to attract better-fit clients and referrals than being generic.

    Related: Target Audience, Offer

    Learn more: Personal Branding for Beginners

  • Target AudienceDigital Business
    Definition
    The specific group of people a product or message is designed for.
    In plain English
    The exact person you’re talking to when you make or market something.
    Example
    New freelancers in their first year of business.
    Why it matters
    Speaking to a specific audience is more effective than speaking to "everyone."

    Related: Niche, Customer Persona

  • Customer PersonaDigital Business
    Definition
    A semi-fictional profile representing your ideal customer.
    In plain English
    A sketch of your dream customer — their situation, frustrations, and goals.
    Example
    "Amara, 27, new freelancer, overwhelmed by pricing her services."
    Why it matters
    Makes marketing and product decisions more concrete and less guesswork-based.

    Related: Target Audience

    Learn more: Customer Research Worksheet

  • Lead MagnetDigital Business
    Definition
    A free resource offered in exchange for someone’s contact information.
    In plain English
    A free freebie that gets someone onto your email list.
    Example
    A free checklist offered in exchange for an email address.
    Why it matters
    Builds an audience you can reach directly, outside of social media algorithms.

    Related: Sales Funnel, Email Marketing

  • Sales FunnelDigital Business
    Definition
    The path a potential customer travels from first hearing about you to buying.
    In plain English
    The journey from "never heard of you" to "just bought your thing."
    Example
    Social post → free guide → email sequence → paid offer.
    Why it matters
    Understanding the path helps you see where potential customers are dropping off.

    Related: Lead Magnet, Conversion Rate

  • SEOMarketing
    Definition
    Search Engine Optimization — improving content so it ranks higher in search results.
    In plain English
    Making sure Google actually shows your stuff when people search for it.
    Example
    Using the phrase "freelance rate calculator" naturally throughout a relevant page.
    Why it matters
    Good SEO brings in visitors without paying for ads.

    Related: SEM, Content Marketing

    Learn more: Digital Skills for Beginners

  • SEMMarketing
    Definition
    Search Engine Marketing — paid advertising that appears in search results.
    In plain English
    Paying to show up at the top of search results instead of earning it organically.
    Example
    A sponsored listing above the regular Google search results.
    Why it matters
    Can bring faster visibility than SEO, at a direct cost per click.

    Related: SEO

  • CTAMarketing
    Definition
    Call To Action — the specific next step you want someone to take.
    In plain English
    The "now click this" moment at the end of a post, email, or page.
    Example
    "Download the free checklist" button.
    Why it matters
    Without a clear CTA, people often just... leave.

    Related: Conversion Rate

  • Conversion RateMarketing
    Definition
    The percentage of people who take a desired action out of everyone who saw the opportunity.
    In plain English
    Out of everyone who saw your offer, how many actually said yes.
    Example
    100 visitors, 5 purchases = a 5% conversion rate.
    Why it matters
    Tells you how effective your offer and messaging actually are, not just how much traffic you get.

    Related: CTA, Sales Funnel

  • Engagement RateMarketing
    Definition
    A measure of how much an audience interacts with content, relative to its reach.
    In plain English
    How much people actually like, comment, and share — not just scroll past.
    Example
    A post with high comments relative to views has strong engagement.
    Why it matters
    Often matters more to algorithms and real influence than raw follower count.
  • Email MarketingMarketing
    Definition
    Marketing directly to people through email.
    In plain English
    Talking to your audience in their inbox instead of hoping they see your post.
    Example
    A weekly newsletter with tips and an occasional offer.
    Why it matters
    You own your email list — unlike a social platform, it can’t change the rules on you overnight.

    Related: Lead Magnet

  • Content MarketingMarketing
    Definition
    Attracting and keeping an audience by consistently creating valuable content.
    In plain English
    Teaching or entertaining people for free until they trust you enough to buy.
    Example
    A series of free how-to videos related to a paid course.
    Why it matters
    Builds trust and visibility before ever asking for a sale.

    Related: SEO

  • Affiliate MarketingMarketing
    Definition
    Earning a commission by promoting someone else’s product.
    In plain English
    You recommend something, someone buys through your link, you get a cut.
    Example
    A blog post recommending tools, each with a tracked affiliate link.
    Why it matters
    A way to earn income without creating your own product.

    Related: Affiliate Link

  • Social Media MarketingMarketing
    Definition
    Using social platforms to promote a brand, product, or service.
    In plain English
    Using Instagram, TikTok, etc. on purpose to grow a business, not just for fun.
    Example
    A consistent content calendar built around specific content pillars.
    Why it matters
    Meets audiences where they already spend time.

    Related: Content Marketing

    In plain English

    In plain English

    Example

    Why it matters
    Creates predictable, recurring income instead of constantly chasing new projects.

    Related: Scope of Work

    In plain English
    The written line in the sand for what you’re actually agreeing to do.
    Example
    "3 blog posts, 800 words each, 1 round of revisions."
    Why it matters
    The single biggest protection against scope creep and disputes.

    Related: Proposal, Revision

  • ProposalFreelancing
    Definition
    A document outlining a project’s scope, timeline, and price, sent to a potential client.
    In plain English
    Your pitch, in writing, for exactly what you’d do and what it costs.
    Example
    A one-page PDF outlining a website redesign project.
    Why it matters
    Sets clear expectations before any work or money changes hands.

    Related: Scope of Work

  • InvoiceFreelancing
    Definition
    A formal request for payment for completed work.
    In plain English
    The official "please pay me now" document.
    Example
    Invoice #004, due in 7 days, sent via email.

    A professional record protects both you and the client.

    Learn more: How to Get Paid as a Freelancer

  • DepositFreelancing
    Definition
    An upfront partial payment made before work begins.
    In plain English
    The "I’m serious" payment a client makes before you start.
    Example
    A 50% deposit due before the project timeline starts.
    Why it matters
    Protects your time investment and filters out non-serious inquiries.

    Related: Invoice, Milestone

  • MilestoneFreelancing
    Definition
    A specific checkpoint in a project, often tied to a partial payment.
    In plain English
    A "we’re halfway there" moment, usually with a payment attached.
    Example
    Payment released after the first draft is approved.
    Why it matters
    Breaks risk into smaller, safer chunks for both sides.

    Related: Deposit

  • RevisionFreelancing
    Definition
    A requested change to completed work.
    In plain English
    A "can you tweak this" request after you’ve delivered something.
    Example
    "2 rounds of revisions included" in a proposal.
    Why it matters
    Setting a limit upfront prevents endless, unpaid back-and-forth.

    Related: Scope of Work
