Client Simulator

Practise the hard conversations 💼

Scope, pricing, payment and difficult clients. Rehearse them here, where mistakes cost nothing.

Pick a scenario, answer in your own words or tap an option, and see what happens next. Good answers are rarely harsh: they are clear, kind and specific. Finish three cases to earn the Client Boss badge.

Cases:

  • The $50 website (Pricing): A new client wants a lot, fast, for very little. You can say no to the package and yes to the person. Name what is realistic, offer a smaller honest option, and put the scope and a deposit in writing before you start.
  • Five tiny extras (Scope): The project is going well. Then the "small" requests begin. Scope creep rarely arrives as one big ask. Check requests against the written scope, quote extras separately, and keep the tone friendly while the boundary stays firm.
  • The late invoice (Payment): The work is done. The money has not arrived. Chase early, politely and in writing, with the invoice attached and a specific date. Terms, a deposit and late-fee wording on the invoice make this conversation short.
  • The free test project (Boundaries): A promising lead asks you to prove yourself with unpaid work. A portfolio, a short call or a small paid trial can prove your skill. Free deliverable-level work usually cannot be reused by you but can be used by them.
  • Revision number six (Feedback): The brief keeps changing and the finish line keeps moving. Clear revision limits, a written approval step and a short "what is not working?" question are the antidote to endless feedback loops.
  • Raising your rates (Pricing): A long-term client has been paying the same for two years. A rate rise is a business decision, not a favour. Give notice, state the new price plainly, thank them for the relationship and offer a clear transition date.

Questions people ask

What is the Client Simulator?

A set of short, branching freelance scenarios. You make two decisions in each, see how the client reacts, watch your money, trust and energy meters move, and get a debrief on five skills.

How does the "write your own reply" checker work?

It looks for plain signals in your text, such as mentioning the agreed scope, offering a smaller option or giving a date. It runs in your browser, uses no AI and is practice feedback only.

Are the scenarios based on real situations?

Yes. They are common freelance moments: low budgets, scope creep, late payment, unpaid test work, endless revisions and raising your rates.
